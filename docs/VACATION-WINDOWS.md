# Vacation Windows — when is the best time to take time off in 2026–2029?

**Definition used:** a window of consecutive calendar days containing **zero MLB games
and zero NFL games**. NFL includes the Hall of Fame Game, all preseason, the regular
season, the **Pro Bowl Games (NFL all-star)**, and the postseason through the Super Bowl.

Everything on this page is generated by `scripts/analyze_vacation.py`. Run
`python3 scripts/analyze_vacation.py` to reproduce it. Do not hand-edit.

---

## The short answer

**It depends on one question: do Spring Training games count as "MLB games"?**

They are real games, played by real MLB rosters, broadcast on radio. But they are
exhibitions, and most people asking for "no baseball" do not mean them. Both readings
are computed below.

> **There is no verified 2- or 3-week window with zero MLB and zero NFL games in any
> year 2026–2029, and 2026's 11-day window is already in the past.** The long run
> available every year is between the Conference Championship Sunday and the Pro Bowl
> Games (Tuesday of Super Bowl week) — 8–11 days, never a full two weeks.

**How to read this year by year:**

- **2026** — fully verified end to end. The clean run is **Feb 9 → Feb 19** (after the

  NFL publishes different playoff dates this is the year most likely to shift.
- **2028 / 2029** — ESTIMATED. MLB has confirmed (via its own API) that those
  schedules are unreleased; only the Super Bowl sites are announced.

## Why every strict window sits in late January / early February

A clean day needs no MLB game (the season spans February → late October) and no NFL
game. The NFL's January/February footprint is sparse — Week 17/18, then four
single-weekend playoff rounds plus the Super Bowl — so the **gaps between NFL weekends**
are what produce clean runs:

1. **Conference Championship Sunday → Pro Bowl Games (Tuesday of Super Bowl week):**
   8–11 clean days every year. This is the dependable annual window under strict.
2. **Day after Super Bowl → MLB Spring Training opener:** Feb 2026 only (11 days),
   because Spring Training has opened Feb 19–22 in every recent season while the
   Super Bowl keeps landing later (Feb 14 in 2027).
3. **Week 18 → Wild Card Weekend** and **Wild Card → Divisional** produce 4–6 day
   runs (January), useful for a shorter trip.

## How the estimate for unreleased seasons was built

**The authoritative signal that a season is unreleased:** the MLB Stats API endpoint
`https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/seasons?sportId=1&season=YYYY` returns a season
record for 2026 and 2027 but **an empty `seasons` list for 2028 and 2029**. That is
MLB's own machine-readable statement that those schedules do not exist yet.

Given that, the estimates use the league's published scheduling rules:

- **MLB Spring Training** has opened between Feb 19 and Feb 22 in recent seasons;
  Feb 19 is used as a consistent, conservative (earliest) anchor.
- **MLB Opening Day** has been in late March; Mar 25 is carried forward from 2026/2027.
- **MLB postseason** ends Oct 31 because a World Series Game 7 cannot run past
  Halloween.
- **NFL first game** (Hall of Fame Game) is the Thursday or Wednesday of the first
  full week of August.
- **NFL playoff rounds** are day-of-week stable; the verified 2026-27 layout (Week 18
  Jan 9–10, Wild Card Jan 16–18, Divisional Jan 23–24, Championships Jan 31, Super
  Bowl Feb 14) is translated to each cycle by anchoring on that cycle's Super Bowl.
- **Super Bowl** is the first or second Sunday of February.
- **Pro Bowl Games** are the Tuesday of Super Bowl week (announced 2025-10-22 for
  2026; applied as a pattern to 2027+).

Every estimated row in `data/verified/seasons.json` carries a `basis` field stating the
reasoning, and a `source` field that reads "NOT RELEASED" rather than a fake URL.
