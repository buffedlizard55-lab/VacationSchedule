# Vacation Windows — when is the best time to take time off in 2026–2029?

**Definition used:** a window of consecutive calendar days containing **zero MLB games
and zero NFL games**. NFL includes the Hall of Fame Game, all preseason, the regular
season and the postseason through the Super Bowl.

Everything on this page is generated by `scripts/analyze_vacation.py`. Run
`python3 scripts/analyze_vacation.py` to reproduce it. Do not hand-edit.

## The short answer

**It depends on one question: do Spring Training games count as "MLB games"?**

They are real games, played by real MLB rosters, broadcast on radio. But they are
exhibitions, and most people asking for "no baseball" do not mean them. Both readings
are computed below.

> **There is no 1-, 2- or 3-week window with zero MLB and zero NFL games in 2027, 2028
> or 2029.** The best available is 2029 at exactly 7 days. 2026 had an 11-day window
> (Feb 9–19) but it is already in the past.

**2029 is the best future year** under this reading, because Super Bowl LXIII falls
latest (Feb 11) while MLB's estimated Spring Training start stays at ~Feb 19.

## Why every window sits in February

A clean day needs to be after the previous NFL season ends and before the next MLB
season begins. Three facts squeeze that gap into roughly one week:

1. **The Super Bowl is always the first or second Sunday of February.**
2. **MLB Spring Training games start around Feb 19–22.**
3. **The NFL Hall of Fame Game is early August**, so the NFL never encroaches on
   February — the whole constraint is the Super Bowl date.

The window is therefore `Super Bowl + 1 day` → `MLB Spring Training start − 1 day`,
and its length is set almost entirely by which Sunday the Super Bowl lands on.

Under the regular-season reading the window simply extends to MLB Opening Day
(~Mar 24/25), which is why those windows are 5–6 weeks.

## How the estimate for unreleased seasons was built

**The authoritative signal that a season is unreleased:** the MLB Stats API endpoint
`https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/seasons?sportId=1&startSeason=2026&endSeason=2029`
returns a season record for **2026 only**. Requests spanning 2027–2029 return no
records for those years. That is MLB's own machine-readable statement that those
schedules do not exist yet.

Given that, the estimates use the league's published scheduling rules:

- **MLB Spring Training** has opened between Feb 19 and Feb 22 in recent seasons;
  Feb 19 is used as a consistent, conservative (earliest) anchor.
- **MLB Opening Day** has fallen between Mar 20 and Mar 31; 2026 was Wed Mar 25, so
  Mar 25 is carried forward.
- **MLB postseason** ends Oct 31 because a World Series Game 7 cannot run past
  Halloween.
- **NFL first game** (Hall of Fame Game) has landed on the Thursday or Wednesday of
  the first full week of August; the nearest weekday to Aug 6 matching 2026's
  Thursday is used.
- **Super Bowl** is the first or second Sunday of February.

Every estimated row in `data/verified/seasons.json` carries a `basis` field stating
the reasoning, and a `source` field that reads "NOT RELEASED" rather than a fake URL.
