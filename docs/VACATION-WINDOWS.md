## The short answer

**Two questions decide it: does Spring Training count as "an MLB game", and do the
Earthquakes count as "busy"?**

1. **If Spring Training counts** — it is real MLB baseball, and Giants Spring Training
   games are broadcast on KNBR — then **no section gives you two weeks in any year.**
   The best you get is the 8–11 days between Conference-Championship Sunday and the
   Pro Bowl Games.
2. **If only the regular season and postseason count**, Sections 1 and 2 open a
   **5–6 week corridor** from the day after the Super Bowl to Opening Day.
3. **Section 3 is the only section that changes the trip.** Adding the Earthquakes
   collapses that corridor to **12 days**, because MLS plays on weekends from late
   February and every 7-day window contains a weekend.

## Why every strict window sits in late January / early February

A clean day needs no MLB game (the season spans February → late October) and no NFL
game. The NFL's January/February footprint is sparse — Week 17/18, then four
single-weekend playoff rounds plus the Super Bowl — so the **gaps between NFL weekends**
are what produce clean runs:

1. **Conference Championship Sunday → Pro Bowl Games (Tuesday of Super Bowl week):**
   8–11 clean days every year, in every section.
2. **Day after Super Bowl → MLB Spring Training opener:** Feb 2026 only (11 days),
   because Spring Training has opened Feb 19–22 in every recent season while the Super
   Bowl keeps landing later (Feb 14 in 2027).
3. **Week 18 → Wild Card Weekend** and **Wild Card → Divisional** produce 4–6 day
   runs (January), good for a shorter trip.

**Why September–December never appears in these tables.** The NFL plays every Sunday,
so no run of seven consecutive days inside the NFL season can be free of NFL games.
The analysis therefore blocks that stretch as one span. It is conservative *and*
provably harmless: `test_no_week_long_run_inside_the_nfl_season` asserts that every
run of 7+ days ends before the Hall of Fame Game. The same argument applies to college
football (Saturdays) and MLS (weekends), which is why those seasons are blocked as
spans too.

## What each section actually costs you

* **Adding Stanford and Cal costs nothing.** Sections 1 and 2 have the same longest run
  in all four years under both readings. Every Stanford and Cal game date already falls
  inside the MLB season frame or the NFL season span; the only days college football adds
  are the early-January bowl window (conditional on qualifying), which never creates or
  destroys a week.
* **Adding the Earthquakes costs a whole trip.** 44 → 12 days in 2026, 38 → 12 in 2027,
  40 → 12 in 2028, 41 → 12 in 2029 (regular-season reading). If a 2- or 3-week vacation
  matters, plan against Section 1 or 2, or accept that an Earthquakes match will be on
  KNBR while you are away.
* **The reading of "no MLB games" matters more than the section does.** Strict: every
  section maxes out at 8–11 days. Regular-season-only: Sections 1–2 give five to six
  weeks.
